**Source Reliability Rubric**

Quality-control document for external research

Version 0.1 | 05/06/2026 | For internal pilot use

# 1\. Purpose

This rubric helps the agent and reviewers assess the reliability of sources used in market, patent, publication, company, contact and funding research.

# 2\. Confidence levels

| **Confidence** | **Source types**                                                                                                                                               | **Use in report**                                           |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| High           | Primary company websites; patent database records; peer-reviewed publications; government, regulator, funder or standards-body pages; official annual reports. | Use as evidence for factual claims. Cite directly.          |
| Medium         | University news pages; reputable trade press; industry associations; credible market commentary; official event pages; investor presentations.                 | Use with caution. Prefer corroboration.                     |
| Low            | Unsourced blogs; vendor marketing without evidence; old or undated pages; unclear authorship; AI-generated summaries; forum posts.                             | Use only as weak signal or exclude. Mark as low confidence. |

# 3\. Minimum evidence standards

- Every market size, trend, company activity, product claim, patent, publication or contact recommendation should have a source.
- Prefer primary sources over secondary commentary.
- For important claims, seek at least two independent sources where possible.
- Do not use unsupported market-size figures as facts.
- If evidence is weak, mark the claim as an assumption or recommended follow-up.

# 4\. Reference capture format

| **Field**              | **Required content**                                                  |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Source title           | Title of page, report, patent, publication or document.               |
| Publisher/organisation | Company, patent office, journal, university, funder or website owner. |
| URL                    | Direct URL where available.                                           |
| Date                   | Publication date if available; otherwise date accessed.               |
| Relevance              | One sentence explaining why the source matters.                       |
| Confidence             | High, medium or low.                                                  |

# 5\. Red flags

- No author, no date and no publisher information.
- Claims copied across multiple websites without a primary source.
- Numbers that appear precise but lack methodology.
- Companies claiming "world first" or "unique" without patent/publication/product evidence.
- Outdated sources in fast-moving markets.
